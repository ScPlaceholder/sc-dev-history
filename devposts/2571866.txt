Unfortunately it’s not as simple as just displacing the vertices to the desired locations, the use of heightmaps allows some massive performance savings in many areas. For example, the physics engine can simply check a height value for collision detection, and textures can be vertically projected; avoiding complex unwrapping or multiple texture projections requiring blending of multiple texture layers.
Our current approach for non heightmap geometry is to just build 3D meshes in the traditional way, and scatter these on the terrain using relatively simple procedural logic (% chance, slope, etc), but placing much larger meshes procedurally is much more complex as they intersect large areas of terrain and this needs to look natural.
My personal opinion is we should link the location of these large meshes to the heightmaps, so that they always appear together (completely skipping the need for procedural placement). This is the equivalent of modelling the terrain without any heightmap limitations, and using the heightmap for the areas that make sense, and meshes for where heightmaps aren’t suitable. However, our heightmaps are actually stretched and distorted slightly so that they fit onto a sphere, and this is a problem for the rigid meshes as we don’t support such distortion on them. To overcome this I’m hoping we can ‘pin’ the two most extreme points of the meshes to the heightmap terrain and just use rotation and scaling to fit the mesh in approximately the right location and accept some minor inaccuracies. The other problem with this approach of linking meshes to heightmaps is that we are constantly blending multiple terrain heightmaps, and there‘s no obvious way to blend a mesh. So this is an open/unsolved topic for us at present.
