Hello everyone! I'm Francesco, and I'm responsible for the new footstep implementation in the game. Thank you all for your interest in the audio of the game, we really appreciate it :slightly_smiling_face:
Allow me to clarify a few points that didn't fully come through in the ATV due to the little time we had to show and talk about our audio improvements:
- this implementation is completely independent from and doesn't rely on the Listener Focus tech that we've been developing recently. This is a much simpler setup, and didn't require any additional code support;
- this is not really a feature, but rather a mixing decision! We make many of these choices on a daily basis, and most of them actually go unnoticed if we don't talk about them in the ATVs :slightly_smiling_face: In fact, a more rudimentary version of this 'focus' feature has been in place since at least alpha 3.0, I wonder if anyone noticed it before!
- even if there are some psychoacoustic considerations involved (which we always take into account when working on the sound experience of the game), this mixing decision is indeed based on actual sound physics. Your ears' pinnas have the ability to funnel sound waves and direct them to your ear canals, and they’re oriented towards the front. Take a look at this article here: https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/ears-do-their-design-size-and-shape-matter/ (it's the first one I found from a quick search on Google, I'm sure there are plenty of other sources available). It clearly states that 'Whereas sounds from the front and sides are enhanced by the pinna, those coming from the back are reduced.' Not only that, but when you look down to your feet your head protrudes to the front, thus decreasing the obstruction caused by your body and allowing a more direct path for your footsteps sounds to reach your ears. If you're not familiar with obstruction, I suggest that you take a look at this page from the Wwise documentation https://www.audiokinetic.com/library/edge/?source=Help&id=understanding_obstruction, where they say that 'When obstruction occurs, the listener hears the reflections of the sound clearly, but the direct path of the sound is obscured. This alteration to the sound is performed through the application of a reduction in volume, an increase in LPF (Low Pass Filter, ed.), or both.' In case you’re in the obstruction topic there are plenty of other resources in the internet. As you can see, these two points (pinnas orientation and obstruction) prove that my work on the footsteps system is bringing them one step closer to a more realistic and a more immersive sound experience in the game. It's also worth noting that the volume variation I introduced when looking down is quite subtle and hardly noticeable. But that's the whole point of it, it has to sound natural and you shouldn't be able to notice it! :slightly_smiling_face:
As @TheJopp correctly mentioned, there are also some selective auditory considerations that apply to this mixing choice. It is true that if you want you can focus on certain sounds and be able to distinguish them in a busy mix, but a well mixed entertainment product helps you to do so with lots of subtle choices, while still sounding realistic. To properly mix the sound for movies and video games is an incredibly hard job, where sometimes the most realistic sounding result is not the one achieved by perfectly replicating what happens in real life. If you're interested in knowing more about this specific topic, I strongly recommend that you watched this brilliant GDC talk about how the Gears of War 4 audio team at Microsoft achieved the most natural sounding reverbs for that title https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024008/-Gears-of-War-4.
Concerning @BloodDragon's comment about surround sound, we did improve Foley's surround mix a lot for this upcoming 3.3 release. This specific mix choice however is not strictly dependent on the channel configuration of the user, and works great on all possible setups. Implementing an HRTF on the footsteps sounds would have achieved, I believe, quite a similar result to what we have obtained with the system you saw in the video. The advantage of the current setup compared to using an HRTF plugin is that it's far less expensive on the CPU, and it provides us with much more control on the sound so we can fine tune it to perfection.

What may feel like a non-essential feature in sound, when summed up with lots of other non-essential features, will create a deeply detailed and immersive experience without giving up on realism, which remains one of our ultimate goals for the audio design aesthetic of the game.
