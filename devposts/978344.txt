It's not realism just for the sake of realism.
For example the purpose of the GForce mechanic is to reward skillful pilots by allowing them to unlock the full potential of any ship they fly.
Because any ship should become a threat in the hands of a good pilot.
In the 3.0 implementation, the GSafe mode allows a 'safe' amount of GStress build-up, which is why you still get some redout/blackout even in GSafe mode, but you shouldn't pass out.
Later on, we plan to expand this mode and use different safety thresholds for different types of ships:
- Civillian transport ships - very safe - allow no GStress build-up.
- Cargo/industrial ships - safe - allow low GStress build-up.
- Military ships - not-so-safe - allow moderate GStress build-up.
We can either use these presets, or allow manual configuration of the GSafe mode. We haven't decided yet.
I recommend experimenting with this mechanic, and learning how to take advantage of it.
Here are some pointers:
- GStress can accumulate in any of the 6 relative directions (up/down/left/right/front/back).
- You will notice the effects when accelerating in the same direction for a longer time.
- The simplest way to reduce GStress is to roll 90 or 180 degrees, depending on your next intended maneuver.
- The most common mistake I've seen is accelerating in one direction, rolling 180 degrees and then accelerating in the opposite direction - this only makes things worse.
I can't go into detail with all the game mechanics we have, but I assure you there's a similar thought process behind everything we do.
If you just want to admire the surrounding environment I recommend using the decoupled mode, or simply avoid taking sharp turns at high speeds.
