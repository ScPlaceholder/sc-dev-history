I describe the use of "fixups", where at times a thruster might be given an in-simulation position that's different from its physical position on the ship, but the expectation is that this difference will be relatively small.  As far as I know, we have never needed to add any hidden thrusters to get a ship to fly.  It doesn't take too much to provide for the primary control axes on a ship.
I suppose it's possible you're referring to control moment gyros, which are actually hidden torque generators which are intended to be non-physicalized, so that as long as the cockpit is intact, you'll have these units to provide a low level of rotational control.  If that's what you mean, then yes, this is true of all ships.  You have 20% of your maximum rotational torque available even if all thrusters get destroyed.
But when it comes to gimbaled thrusters, the issue there is that the thruster will begin to generate force on the goal vector before the thruster has finished rotating into that vector.  So when you start to rotate in the Hornet, you will see a very controlled rotation that doesn't properly correlate with the vector of the thrusters generating it.  I'm not a fan of it, but that's the only way to make these types of thrusters work, as I've described in the doc.
