It affects how your real world head position and rotation affects the ingame head position. By default all positional head movements you do in front of your screen (forward, back, left, right, up, down) are executed based on where your actor is looking at.
Lets say you look 10 degrees to the right in the real world and your avatar looks 25 degrees right in the game world. If you then just forward towards the screen, you ingame avatar won't lean forwards, it will instead lean 25 degrees to the right (using the view direction as reference). That is to make moving around in the cockpit a bit easer and keeping a steady frame of reference.
The new option however allows to disconnect that rotation-position relationship. So when you lean forward in the real world you actually lean forward in the game world. So it more translates the real position of your head. If you have a TrackIR this option doesn't exist but you can disable the similar behaviour in the external TrackIR tool. The option is called "TrueView".
That kind of movement is a bit more inconvenient for the average head tracking user but likely very helpful for people who build custom solutions or even go for fake VR solutions as it keeps the head position in the right place.
