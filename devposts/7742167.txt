I'll add some more details/context to my previous reply to help answer some of the questions in this thread, but please lets all place nicely, I only post here with very open answers because I enjoy the interactions with you all.
When I said SC hasn't taken a full drop of the engine, that means:
- No full drop of the combined main branch, which includes every system, not just graphics
- Individual changes are integrated into SC all the time, if they are sufficiently de-coupled and thoroughly tested
- Lots of development continues directly within the SC branch, particularly with stability, performance, QoL, and various new features for upcoming releases
The graphics changes in the 'main' branch include many stability and performance improvements to Vulkan, including multi-threading, HDR, shader optimisations, and more. However they are also intertwined with many other parts of the code-base, such as wider rendering changes and profiling/memory-management changes that touch every system. So the Vulkan changes can't trivially be integrated into SC without the rest. It would be possible to manually integrate just the renderer and then undo all the unrelated changes, but this would probably push back our overall schedule by several weeks (and likely miss deadlines for Planet Tech V5, Ray Tracing, Squadron 42, etc), so normally we wait for SC to take the full main branch.
So while a lot of work has gone into making the main branch of Vulkan faster and more stable, the rest of the main branch may not be, and the company needs to take careful judgement about when to integrate, how long to test, and how much risk they are prepared to introduce to each release. Also, it's worth noting that while Vulkan seems faster and more stable for us internally, the true test is always when it goes to the wider public with a much broader mix of hardware, drivers and software.
Someone asked about the possibility of a Vulkan/tech preview, and this is something we're actively considering, but again this costs time and effort, which pushes back our overall schedule/progress because we have to either integrate and de-couple just the renderer, or we need to take the whole of 'main' and then we could end up with problems in unrelated systems that we (graphics) cannot fix without pulling people off other potentially higher priority work (e.g. server stability, QoL improvements). So a preview channel only becomes worthwhile if we suspect there would be a prolonged delay to getting graphics/tech updates to the players.
But as I mentioned previously, we are also exploring ways we can try to keep features/system improvements more isolated to give management more options as to when to integrate each feature/system into SC, such that feature A doesn't block feature B.
I can appreciate that some of this can be frustrating to hear for people who are looking for improvements to Vulkan in the short term, but trust me it's equally frustrating for us developers! It's far easier to take a gung ho approach, but that has been proven in several past releases to not lead to a good player experience, which ultimately hurts the game and the company.
