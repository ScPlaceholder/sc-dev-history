There are still some questions surrounding how we handle the transition between zones (interior/exterior, ship/planet) that we haven't got a final answer for yet, so I can't be 100% sure of how this will look, but yes I'm hoping this will become a thing of the past!
Unlike most games, we have the very problematic scenario of complex lighting setups on moving space-ships, and we need to ensure no unexpected light leaks outside the ship, but can't always rely on shadow maps (can be costly, and not supported on our environment probes at all). As a result we end up relying on a system which clamps the effects of a light to a specific zone or vis-area, and that is what you're seeing above. If we disabling this clamping in the current lighting tech you'd get some even more ugly lighting issues, with lights bleeding outside the ship, but also the interior of the ship would become very washed out with the exterior planet/space-station lighting, even when the doors are closed.
With the new lighting system, rather than directly applying unshadowed environment probes to entire rooms, we fire rays from every pixel on the screen (either hardware accelerated or software) to gather the correct lighting on a much finer scale. What is unclear at present is how the rays will pass from one zone to another, whether they'll pass directly, indirectly, or whether we'll implement a kind of 'portal' that collects and passes light between zones... but whichever solution we choose, the intention is that light can freely pass between zones, and so opening a ship's ramp will result in the exterior lighting flooding in.
