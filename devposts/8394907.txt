What we showed back then was that the nose steers in the direction of the airflow over the airframe. But that's not a purposeful control, that's just the aerodynamics acting on the ship frame.
The ability or inability to point the nose down during a hover depends on the thrust of the thrusters against gravity. NFM will be a bit harsher in cutting down the thruster efficiencies so you really feel the differences in atmo. E.g. in our current NFM tuning the vertical Gs of a Gladius in standard Earth atmo are restricted to somewhat around 1.5Gs ... so that's still enough to take off vertically without using boost but it's not a lot of buffer (you're left with 0.5G to climb, ships with VTOLs have an advantage here). If you put your ship nose down, you then start relying on the often weakest thrust direction against gravity and you might have a harder time holding that position ... if a ship is heavily loaded, the thruster damaged or the power assignment too low, the ship might not be able to hold position at all. But also that is just "current" tuning and we will have differences between ships.
So going nose down in a hover during combat (to e.g. attack infantry on the ground) makes you more vulnerable as you have a much harder time to evade with the weakened thrusters. For other things like mining VTOLs are your friend and we also want to get rid of the requirement to constantly go nose down during landings. But we'll talk more about this later.
Hope that clears it up.
