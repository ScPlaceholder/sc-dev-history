Fundamentally the decision was that we needed to move to one of the current-gen graphics APIs, be that Vulkan or D3D12 (or Metal, but we're not on Mac). Vulkan is theoretically faster that D3D11 because it moves a lot of stuff that happened "by magic" in D3D11 out of the realm of magic and makes it the developer's problem. So the initial stage of a Vulkan port most likely runs slower and has more broken features, but the performance work is more able to address the root cause of a problem, rather than having to work around a load of stuff in a black box.
Besides performance though, there are features (most notably ray-tracing) that aren't available in D3D11, so we needed the upgrade.
As to why we're not doing a D3D12 mode as well, it's a question of whether there's a benefit to doing it. D3D12 is very different from D3D11, arguably closer to Vulkan, so there's not a straightforward upgrade path from our D3D11 code. We have people who could write the D3D12 back-end from scratch, but they're already writing the Vulkan back-end.
