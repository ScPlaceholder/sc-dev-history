The biggest linguist influences are Thai, Mandarin, and Japanese. For example, the sound system mimics many things that go on in those languages kind of all mashed together in its own mindset. Both Japanese and Thai have long vowel distinctions. Both Thai and Mandarin use tone in some ways that are similar. But, grammatically speaking, I doubt anyone who speaks Japanese or Mandarin on Thai will find that the language feels familar to them. Also, it has some sounds in it, like THL (thla, thlo, thlu, thli, thle) that are very ALIEN to all of these languages. There are also some tongue-in-cheek ironies going on between the way some of the words sound (to speakers of European languages) and what they actually mean in Xi’an. ma’ma meaning something like "beast" is a good example of that. But, even ma’ma has a legitimate derivation within the language's framework. It's not just completely arbitrary. This was also partially inspired by the Mandarin "tone game" sentence: 妈妈骂马吗? “Did mother scold the horse?" In Xi’an the raw "elemental" tai which is ma represents "animal" (vs. plant (ii)). The word for "fauna" is ma’a. The slang term "beast" came from ma e m,a, which means "her/his animal" in the Service Dialect. When someone is behaving like a "beast" that's their "internal animal" coming out. I want the language to be fun, so let's make it so! It's fine for me for that to happen as nerdish-ly as possible. :slightly_smiling_face:
