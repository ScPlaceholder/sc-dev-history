To me, what they are saying in RTV is, that there exists a problem of unpredictabilty in audio design in modern games and they are absolutely right about that. In music or movies you can be very precise and deliberate with your mixing, as every sound is fixed in time and space (no pun intended) - in other words: predictable. You do not have that luxury in an interactive medium like games, where a sound could originate from any point at any time and in any amount of different conditions. That rings especially true with SC, where we have different atmospheric properties. So unless you want mushing and droning from your speakers in a heated battle with multiple explosions, guns blazing etc., you need to be selective in what you put into the sub bass/bass range and when.
They did not say that there will be no low end at all. They even mention authoring discreet audio only send to the sub in surround setups, to not clutter the lower end. If anything, this will improve the experience for people with hi-fi and high-end setups. But they will have to devise a system to better control what occupies the low end. They also mention the problem of a big low end in metering (i. e. making sure there is no signal clipping). Having a lot of low frequency will add volume to an audio signal without carrying much perceivable information, which leaves less room for other sounds.
