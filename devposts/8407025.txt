That also fits well for optimisations like Foveated Rendering too. That's where you reduce the rendering complexity of areas in your peripheral vision. Your fovea is the very central part of your retina where you have a very dense collection of cells able to make out details. Humans have a surprisingly wide field of view (about 220°), but you can only make out details over a very small portion of that area from your foveal view and surrounding areas, creating your "macular vision". This is approximately 6° wide. So if you can render everything outside of that range with a more simplistic model you can make some great savings. Of course it then raises other challenges: it doesn't work if  you're livestreaming, your eye tracking needs to be up to speed, you need to make sure the objects in the peripheral view don't 'pop' or look inconsistent, because you peripheral vision is excellent at detecting motion, etc. But it's an interesting concept.
