Your translations are excellent! e ha” to’ath e xān lē! Please keep in mind that these were from an early prototype of the language and some things may have changed slightly since December of last year. The word *yeth’thla was originally meant to mean "spouse" but it does not exist any more after more discussion in the writing team on Xi’an families. That word in its modern and "permanent" form is nyā’i. And re: number 4, I'd probably rewrite the first part as lo S.un’ath hai e no’a... . #7 is about "piloting." #8 might mean "Did you try to make a joke?" but in that case it would more likely be with chi intead of pa, so good job on the sense of pa here. In #9, I should have written yō instead of just yo to be a bit more proper, but It's fully intelligible the way it is and the translation is great. The translation of #10 is very good, but I might render it more simply as "Thloan is very good at telling very bad jokes." Another note about using o here instead of e. When these sentences were originally composed as proof of concept, e did not exist in the language yet. The form o to’ath still exists, but it implies that Thloan is really working hard at something that is not naturally one of his talents. It's a subtlety in the language that might be hard to recognize at first. When someone is naturally good at something or they have worked hard to perfect their skills then they e to’ath.
