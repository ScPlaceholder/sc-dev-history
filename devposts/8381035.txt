Ah. Mirrors and the like can be fickle beasts. They are what's known as Render To Texture (RTT) effects. Essentially a second render camera is placed in the world, then draws the environment from the mirror's point of view, and writes it to an image buffer. Then when your player camera render the scene for you, it applies the image buffer onto the mirror surface (or camera feed etc). This brings some limitations with it. For example some particle effects are updated on the GPU when they are drawn, so if they are rendered twice, they can get a double update. In general, at the moment, particles don't draw in RTT renders because of the issues it can cause (we do plan to fix this soon though).
RTTs are automatically more expensive, because instead of rendering the scene once, you're now drawing it once for every mirror in addition to your main frame. If you're in a comms call with another player, that's another RTT pass, so you're now rendering three times. You can imagine how t can grow (and why in general you're rarely see two mirrors facing each other in a video game). As I understand it, as a performance improvement, sometimes we avoid rendering some RTTs if you are low on system resources, eg. if there is not enough available GPU memory for more textures, or frame rate is already low.
This is possibly what's happening for you. After all, the Polaris is a big ship needing lots of memory as it is. It's hard o diagnose remotely and may be something different, but that's my guess.
