Players will be spending hours and hours in the audio environments of SC  ships, whereas the audience was only exposed to the Falcon's  environment for seconds at a time, and what sounds good for 10 seconds  might get really irritating after a couple of hours.
Nailed it. :stuck_out_tongue: You only hear a film once, and it is the same every time. A game... well, you know the rest! You might have a Star Citizen play session where you're continually piloting your ship for the length of a typical movie.
This is a fundamental difference between film sound and game sound, and a reason why it is unfair to compare the two as apples to apples.
Various tricks can be employed like playing these more verbose sounds just after starting the ship up for the first time that session, before slowly fading them back. We do the same with gradually ducking down the cockpit ambience when there has been no thruster activity for a while (which is probably what you're experiencing when you say:
In SC, if a ship is just sitting still it makes very little sound.
Perhaps we duck it too severely, and/or need to think of more ways to dynamically adjust the mix of the various elements.
The goal of game audio designers is to be able to achieve the sort of narrative/immersive effect that film sound can achieve, but to do it in an interactive setting, which is where the huge challenge lies :slightly_smiling_face: We've made huge strides in the past ~2 years with regard to Star Citizen's audio, but there's still a lot more to be done.
Have to say I really appreciate people caring about the tiny nuanced details too :slightly_smiling_face: We definitely put that sort of love and detail into our ambient audio design, but perhaps we're playing it back too quietly, or not allowing it to be as verbose as, say, the Falcon when it needs to be.
