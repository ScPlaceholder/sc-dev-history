There is a big section in the Language Overview Document on Xi’an names. They do not follow Chinese or other Asian conventions strictly per se. In Xi’an culture your mother or your mother’s Line gives you a personal name. In everyday life a Xi’an citizen's "profession" (role in society) takes on the significance of a "family name" and the family names are not discussed commonly or openly in polite conversation. Your friends and colleagues will refer to you by your given name or a nickname. The Xi’an do not care (as a general rule) about human family names (provenance) because that is all completely outside of their cultural context. They care about what you DO and they need an easy-for-them-to-pronouce given name or nickname by which they will call you once you have an established relationship. You might note that the Letter from T.ai in the Language Overview Document is signed simply "T.ai". His "title" at the Xi’an Imperial Academy and his personal name are important. His lineage is not something that they would emphasize in this context, even though it is quite formal.
