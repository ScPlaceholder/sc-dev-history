Correct.  Since we don't have realistic orbital mechanics, there's no reason why you need to enter atmosphere at a high velocity and use atmosphere as a brake, causing burn-in.  And if that's the case, the only danger to the pilot is lack of oxygen.  So if  you have your flight suit, you'll be fine.  Any burn-in you experience will just be for show, since it's currently based on your distance from the surface rather than the actual speed of the vehicle relative to atmosphere.  If it was based on the accurate burn-in model, bringing your hover vehicle into atmosphere at a couple hundred meters per second wouldn't cause any more problems than flying just above the surface at a couple hundred meters per second.  There isn't anything magical about the barrier between space and atmosphere.  It's just a gradual increase in atmospheric pressure the closer you get to the planet.
