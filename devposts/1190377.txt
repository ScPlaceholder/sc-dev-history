The emergence of a digital economy has provided organized crime with a method with which they are able to launder illicitly obtained money. This typically includes compromising video game accounts and using them to sell in game currency for real money. I'll skip over the specifics of the different types of account compromises since it doesn't pertain to this particular question.
With regards to this question, the important thing that we have to look at is the fact that players accounts do get compromised in a number of different games and when that happens, a video game company has to make a decision. Do they help players that have had their account compromised or not? Without getting too much into the specifics, most MMO companies that I'm aware of have chosen to help players that have been through this and I'm glad that's the case because it's a stressful and emotional event that is best to not have to experience (I've investigated a fair number of these at a previous job, they suck ). Because of this, it's typical for companies to employ workflows that help aid customers in protecting their accounts and provide some additional peace of mind.
What it comes down to is that the more of these that a company can prevent, the better of an experience they can provide to their players. Given that preventing these account compromises also drastically reduces the amount of work associated with these account compromises, it makes a great sense to implement and support the usage of an optional two factor authentication. It's also typical ( and is the case for several video game companies ) to have specific features or incentives that rely on two factor authentication being in use though the specifics will vary from one company to the next.
