So in the tuning process, the designers first have to select a particular ship hull and loadout to be the reference ship for the tuning.  Then they specify the performance parameters that they want for the ship on each axis.  So for example they can say how long they want it to take for this particular ship to reach maximum velocity on the forward axis, or to stop from maximum velocity on the side axis.  With these parameters provided, they load the reference ship into the game engine and run the auto-tune tool, which calculates the amount of thrust that each thruster must have in order to achieve that performance.  These thruster capacities are locked in for the thrusters on that ship.  Then if later those thrusters are upgraded, this will change the performance of the ship accordingly.  Or if the ship loses part of its mass, either by taking damage or by dropping part of its loadout, or gains mass by taking on cargo or a heavier loadout, this will also change the performance of the ship.  Thruster damage, loss of power, etc., are all ways that the performance of the ship can change once it is tuned.
