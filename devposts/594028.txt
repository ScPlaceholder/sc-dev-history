Q2. Your timescale of when Xi'an reproduce is off. Typically, they come to sexual maturity around age 70, at the end of their required military service. Most wouldn't choose to reproduce right then, either. They might have careers to establish, relationships to navigate, life to live, family politics to navigate, and so on. Lore as a group hasn't yet determined the typical age a Xi'an would reproduce, but I'd put it at around 100. Some Xi'an get lucky and successfully reproduce at around 80. So the scale is more like
- 000 yrs time. A = born (female).
- 100 yrs time. A = mother, B = daughter.
- 200 yrs time. A=grandmother B = mother C = daughter.
- 300 yrs time. A = great-grandmother, B = grandmother, C = mother, D = son.
I also tweaked your list to represent what Xi'an typically track within their families. They live in big, matrilineal families with smaller bondgroups within them. Xi'an males always reproduce outside the family, and are not involved in the life of their biological offspring. This is due to a lot of complicated cultural factors. They ARE involved in the lives of all the children in their family, and serve as uncles and surrogate parents, along with aunts.

Q3a. We haven't decided whether dementia affects Xi'an. I'm leaning towards no.
Q3b. Xi'an have multiple romantic partners at the same time. Sex and love are divorced from one another in their culture. There are many reasons of this that we'll cover at a later date. Long lived elders may change careers. Midlife crisis question uncertain, but could be interesting.
Q3c. Typically, elders are very well regarded. Each large family is more or less under the leadership of a council of three elders. Elders often hold prestigious titles with "Master" appended as a mark of respect for their long life and experience. A Xi'an would regard sending an elder to a home as abhorrent. That's what the big family is for.
