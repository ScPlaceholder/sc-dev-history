As of now, there is no plan for control surfaces to be used for controllability.  What we do plan to have is lifting surfaces which IFCS can use to provide force to augment thrusters.  So a vehicle like a Gladius will have a lifting body plus lifting wings, and will therefore benefit from a velocity-dependent lifting force on the ship's vertical axis.  If the ship is generating sufficient upward lift to counter gravity, then thrusters won't be used (conserving fuel).  But if the ship is not generating sufficient upward lift, thrusters can be used, and this provides our vehicles with supermaneuverability in atmosphere.  A vehicle can achieve maneuvers that an aircraft could not achieve, including stopping in mid-air.
Back when we first introduced the VTOL fans on the Constellation, I pointed out that I could support them as part of the existing flight control system.  However, I was not given that opportunity at that time.  I hope I will in the future.  I would love to see those fans operational.
Ships like the Gladius and Sabre will definitely benefit from smaller drag resistance on their aerodynamic axis.  And as I stated before, they will benefit from lifting surfaces.  Also, as with thrusters, lifting surfaces can be damaged or lost, causing the vehicle to have degraded or lost lifting force.
It's not a true and complete aerodynamic simulation, but I've tried to capture the details that are important to our needs.  Most importantly, the cross-sectional area and center of drag of the ship is dynamic.  As mass distribution, center of mass and moments of inertia affect a ship's performance in space, changing cross-sectional area and center of drag along with changes in mass distribution, etc., will affect how ships perform in atmosphere.
