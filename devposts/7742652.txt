There are no easy answers here. The people that can most easily pioneer or take more radical approaches that discard all temporal tech are generally those with smaller/simpler engines or games that don't have to directly compete with all the other AAA games.
If you wanted to take a zero temporal approach, then you'd likely have to acccept no real time GI, glossy reflections, hard/basic shadow filtering, shader aliasing (i.e. not geometric aliasing, which is relatively easy to deal with for larger triangles using SMAA), no Nanite style super-detailed geo. etc. Of course you could find clever approaches/comprimises to achieve some of these, but you'd certainly pay a price in features to achieve this vision. The real question is then whether catering for this relatively 'niche' vision is really going to make your game better overall and get more players/revenue, and there we end up in the land of predictions and subjectivity which I won't argue for/against.
What I can say is that internally most graphics programmers deeply dislike temporal techniques, but we like the features that it enables us to implement which otherwise would might be unachievable for 5-10 years.
