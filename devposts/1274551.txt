This is something that we really needed for the original Cutlass design, where we needed a way to designate a high performance yaw (mains rotated forward and backward) or roll (mains rotated upward and downward).  Those kinds of thruster orientations really need to be defined and selected by the pilot, because the flight system can't really assume when you want to engage this kind of high performance maneuver.  Other lesser thrusters provide for yaw and roll, and by the time the large mains rotate into position to really kick in that extra power, you've already mostly completed the action.  So as you probably remember from the early versions of that ship, those mains just kind of twitched around and never really generated much force.
But well we support it?  If our designers are convinced that it's a good feature.  The main problem we have with this kind of system is just our lack of controller space.  Having a system to define macros is easy enough, but we need to devote controller space to triggering these macros, and that was the main reason we didn't consider some kind of devoted super-yaw or super-roll command for the Cutlass.
