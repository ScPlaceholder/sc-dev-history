But just based on my understanding of physics and how the system actually works, I can do some analysis.  The first thing to realize is that torque shouldn't really be impacted much by the location of the center of mass. Torque is generated by pure torque pairs, or torque couples, and in this case, all that matters is the distance between the two thrusters that are generating the torque.  They could both be on the same side of the center of mass.  You could have a pair of thrusters on the left wing that work together to generate yaw, for example.  However, I need to check this, because I was aware of a condition in which IFCS would ignore torque pairs if they are on the same side of the center of mass, and it's possible that's what's happening here.  I will need to check the history, but I don't believe I actually fixed that bug.  I will make a note of it.
So potentially, what's happening is that the loss of the main thruster on one side forces the center of mass toward the other side, and this puts the remaining thrusters on the same side of COM, causing IFCS to ignore them for available torque.  Then once the other engine is gone, it restores the center of mass to near the center of the ship, allowing those thrusters to function again, and at the same time, because of a greatly decreased moment of inertia, allowing the ship to actually perform better in rotations.
