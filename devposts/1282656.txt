This is talking about one specific filter that is used, where it blends between either matching the movement of the target or matching that movement and also closing on the target.  When you're otherwise getting a strong result from ESP, if you're far from the target, that result will assist you in closing on the target.  But if you're near the target, that result will assist you in maintaining your nose position relative to the target without closing.  This is important because we don't want to force your aim point onto the center of mass of the ship.  We want you to be able to provide input that adjusts the aim point relative to the center of mass of the ship.  This is something you should be able to feel in tests.  When you're on target, it's pretty easy to generally hold your lock on the target as it moves across the screen.  That's a significant part of what ESP is supposed to do.  But you can still adjust your aim over the target as you're tracking, and this allows you to selectively target different areas of the ship, or most importantly, correct your shot when the targeting solution is incorrect, since it can't possibly account for all of the factors that lead to a perfect shot every time.
There are other filters that determine how ESP blends between its ideal input and your true input.  What I'm describing here is how ESP determines what its ideal input will be.
