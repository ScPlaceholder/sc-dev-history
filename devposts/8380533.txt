It's not so much a team that fixes bugs, but the team hat works on a feature, with a little cross team collaboration mixed in.
Say a team implements a new mission type, for example, it's released and discovered there is an issue where collisions break if too many people try to do the mission at the same time. It would be the team who created the mission who are tasked with fixing it. They are likely already starting on the next piece of work, so tasks need shuffling around so they can work on the bugs, and since it's a collision problem, they might need te help of the physics team to resolve it. But they find that the physics team are also working on their own features, so have to negotiate priorities to get some assistance. Then maybe after some investigation, the physics team discover it's not actually a physics problem at all, but a network desynchronisation issue. So now the networking team needs to get involved, but they are (as you can imagine) also very busy, and so on.
Even critical bugs in PU can take some time to uncover and and trigger a chain reaction of teams needing to investigate. Then to verify that it is indeed fixed you need to be able to test it. If it's a bug that can only be reproduced at massive scale, it can be hard to know it's resolved for certain with only internal testing.
