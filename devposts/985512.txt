Speaking only in general terms (and not to the particular actions mentioned), sometimes a project will do a rough pass on a set of animations and work with Programming to get the underlying system to work. Rather than spend a lot of time polishing "Enter Ship-A From Forward Left", we might do a rough pass on several animations to provide Programming with what they need.  When it comes time to do Ship-B, they might re-use the Ship-A animation, which will look wonky, but from a technical standpoint, it "worked"; the character did enter the ship, but he had to teleport 3 feet to get to the new ship's proper entry point.
The idea is that it's better to have a wonky animation than a t-pose or a crash. That leaves multiple departments free to move on and do the next bit of work that is needed.  Programming is free to work on what happens after the character sits down or after he presses a button; the audio department would be able to move forward since now they have a piece of data to see in action.
Generally, this methodology allows Animation to then go back at a later time and provide a more polished version that will just overwrite what is already there and everything looks smoother....hopefully.
Because of all this, as others have suggested, it may take a while to get these files polished up, but it is important that it happen, eventually.
