In my tests where I've graphed out the actual motion of ships for actions like strafe up, strafe down, fly forward, stop, I can see the impact of gravity and drag and they are what would be expected for the levels in these cases.  It's possible that you're seeing different results and that there's a bug.  But in my tests I am getting expected results.
I have a graph of one test I did on a planet with about 1 Earth gravity.
This is an acceleration graph as I strafe up in gravity and atmosphere, stop, then strafe back down and stop.  It shows that as you start to accelerate up in gravity and atmosphere, you hit the maximum available upward acceleration, which is your upward acceleration minus gravity since thrusters are already compensating for that.  Then as you pick up speed, drag increases, so more of your thrust goes to compensate for drag, and the curve tapers down because you have less and less acceleration to keep you increasing velocity.  Then going the other way, as it starts to stop you, it can use gravity to help you stop, so the maximum available deceleration is your downward thrust plus gravity.  Also, since you're moving quickly, you have high drag that's going to help you stop.  As you slow down, that drag decreases, and that's why the acceleration tapers toward zero.
Then as you accelerate downward, it's similar.  The downward acceleration is your downward thruster acceleration plus gravity, and it tapers toward zero as drag increases with downward velocity.  Then as you start to stop, you initially have a high drag force, but that drops off quickly as velocity decreases, and you're left with your upward thrust minus gravity.
I think this curve demonstrates that the system is working properly in this case.  Of course if there are cases where it's failing, I'll get that cleaned up.
