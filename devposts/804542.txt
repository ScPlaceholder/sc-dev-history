While we output to sRGB at the end of our pipeline*, lighting in linear space has been pretty common in most games since about 2007. So what makes this guy's scenes look so nice?
1) Freedom to control light - to make a comparison, look at the quality differences between art photography and TV show lighting. In one, the artist can carefully arrange the subject and lighting tweak the film's colour response etc, to get the perfect shot. In the other, the set designer knows they aren't going to change many things shot-by-shot, so they have to have a space that can be viewed from a lot of angles with the same film. Games tend to strive for cinematic lighting which is somewhere in between (SC being no exception) but very often they're trying to do it under similar constraints to a TV show, since they have no idea where the player's going to point the camera. This tends to mean tweaking down the brightness of the sun and sky for example.
2) Global illumination - Offline renderers are always going to be the king for this. The artistic shots in this video are almost entirely lit from multiple bounces which have been calculated correctly for every ray. SC's global illumination is somewhat behind the state of the art here, we have fairly sparse specular and diffuse probes which are mostly baked offline, this means you don't get the same richness of subtle bounces around objects, but even newer systems tend to have to "cheat" (eg with ambient occlusion) to add in fine detail, since the memory and performance cost to store that fine detail would be absurd.
*the majority of monitors take sRGB input. The story with newer HDR monitors is a little more complicated, since they take a slightly reduced colour space, then reduce it further internally based on hardware specific rules... but that's another story.
