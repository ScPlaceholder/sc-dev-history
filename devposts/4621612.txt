Thanks for tagging me, this thread is quite interesting and I'll get our QA to take a look. However my default response is of skepticism (as with many on the thread!).
Here's some initial thoughts:
- Changing the behavior of V-Sync is often going to give quite a radical changes to both the frame-rate and the percieved smoothness of the game (lag vs seeing all frames vs consistant refresh etc). How the different v-sync options will affect your experience is massively affected by your individual setup, in particular the time it takes to update the game code (CPU), render-submission (CPU) and to render the frame (GPU), and is too complex to summarise all the different possibilities in a short forum post. As a result people should assess any v-sync changes completely seperately from any other graphics changes.
- Shaders go through two levels of 'compilation'. The first is taking the HLSL code and compiling into DirectX's shader language (assembly), and this is initiated by our renderer, but the second is where the graphics driver takes the DirectX assembly and compiles that to GPU specific bytecode for your GPU, and this part is largely beyond our control. We ship a cache of pre-compiled shaders in DirectX assembly with the game, and if extracted this is currently around 1GB (far larger than we'd like!), and this is supposed to cover the vast majority of shaders we require for the game... however it's not guaranteed to cover all required shaders (the required shader list is built by our QA process, so things can sometimes be missed such as old/rare assets nobody has tested for quite a while). For each shader we compile, the driver is likely to compile multiple, because it looks to create optimised/bespoke versions of the shader for use in different scenarios (e.g. using it with different geometry formats, or writing to different texture formats). The drivers compiled shaders are likely to be of a similar size to our own, but as it may compile multiple then it's possible we could end up with say 2+GB of driver cached shaders for the entire game.
- I'd be VERY suprised if caching of shaders made any difference to run-time performance after the first few minutes of initial loading/streaming. After this point we don't expect any shader compiles to take place, and so whether they are cached or not shouldn't change anything. However NVidia don't share the internal details of what they might do inside the driver, so we can never be 100% sure.
- Testing the performance gains from modifying the drivers shader cache size should not be done in PU, because the performance there is frequnently limited by the amount of content/players in your vacinity. To do a proper test you'd need to load a single player mode, wait for say 5 minutes for the shader to stop doing any compilation in the backgroud, then take note of the frame-rate, then repeat the process again after changing the cache size.
- If we (or NVidia) are still indeed compiling shaders after several minutes on first load, or at every location, then this is something we'd want to investigate further.
- We have some early RnD in progress to reduce the number of shaders we use, but this is a longer term goal.
