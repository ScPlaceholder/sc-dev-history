Indeed. Without knowing the shape of the ground surface, you'll have the disc intersecting with the ground. The way a decal volume works is that you draw a 3D volume that contains any area you want to shadow, but also areas you don't. But instead of drawing just the front like a normal object, you draw the back faces, and only where they're behind the existing scene geometry. You can then project the scene depth value to work out where in the blob shadow it falls, and shade it appropriately. This is similar to the way that blood splatters, tire tracks etc are already added to the world. I think there could be issues using them for trees, however. Using those special depth testing rules means that they're not culled very efficiently when, for instance, there's just something big in the foreground blocking your view of them (they're still behind that, so they have to invoke the pixel shader before they can work out that they're too far behind it to need shading), and the exact rules for how to blend together many slightly overlapping blobs isn't clear.
