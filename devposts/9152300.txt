We've actually been tracking this one for some time, indeed, and I recently got an update from the team that I can share with you.
What you're seeing comes down to a difference in how the atmosphere behaves depending on the environment outside the ship.
- In a vacuum, when a room is exposed, it loses most of its atmosphere. However, the gas that remains retains its original temperature. Once the room is sealed again, the Life Support system mainly needs to replace the missing atmosphere, which it can do relatively quickly at its default temperature.
- On a planet, the situation is a little different. As the room loses its atmosphere, some of it can be replaced by the planet's external atmosphere, which can be extremely cold. Once the room is sealed again, there may already be enough gas inside, but that gas now needs to be heated back up.
At the moment, heating a very cold room requires considerably more from the Life Support system than simply replacing missing atmosphere. The system is still working to bring the temperature back up, but the recovery can take much longer and make it look like it's struggling or overloaded. The team is currently looking at that behavior, so that could mean adjusting the tuning so Life Support can heat cold rooms more effectively, or looking at changes to how atmospheric gases behave when entering a ship from the environment, or even other solutions that we're reviewing.
So, in the end: we’re aware of the issue, we’re tracking it, and our teams are investigating the best solution to address what you’re experiencing. :+1:
