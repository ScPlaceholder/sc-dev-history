Hi there Amir. I'm a Lighting Artist in Frankfurt so hopefully i can help answer your question.
Here on Star Citizen, we use our in-house engine for 95% of the lighting process. All game lighting is done in-engine with any required modelling done in 3ds Max, or creating colorgrading LUTs (lookup tables), or projection textures in Photoshop.
I would say that to work in lighting on video games specifically, nothing beats knowledge and experience with game engines. Lumberyard is the closest in UI and tools to our engine, but industry standards usually call for experience with Unreal or Unity. Other studios with their own engines sometimes do all their lighting work directly inside 3ds Max or Maya which then gets exported into the engine. The benefit of using a real-time engine directly though usually means you get much quicker/instant feedback as you move lights around or tweak things.
Just as important though as knowing software, is understanding and practicing knowledge of core art principles like composition, color theory, and shading. So painting and photography/videography are great hobbies that can improve your skills as a lighting artist!
Hope this helps!
