" Does that mean that if I fly decoupled in a straight line there's a chance I keep using power/fuel or generating heat? "
The change in heat, power and fuel is based on acceleration, not velocity.  But that has its own issues.  Gravity definitely throws a wrench into that system.
"Speaking of fuel, is there fuel consumption for flying coupled in a straight line without changing your momentum at all (the thrusters are visibly continuously firing in this case but I can imagine that being 'rule of cool')?"
First, it depends if you're in vacuum or atmosphere.  In vacuum, constant velocity is no different than zero velocity and shouldn't cost anything.  Thrusters are firing because fx tend to like to base these things on velocity rather than acceleration.  But for a simulation, acceleration is king.  But if you're in atmosphere, then yes, it should cost fuel to maintain a constant velocity, because you're pushing against drag.  However, with our system, it does not.  Because fuel cost is based on acceleration, and if thrusters are nullifying drag, there is zero net acceleration and you're maintaining a set velocity.  This is why these things should be based on actual thruster functionality, not just motion.
