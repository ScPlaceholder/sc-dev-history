Previous animated screens have used pre-rendered video, which is captured in-game but saved to disk and streamed in during game-play. The intention is for most/all of these to be live rendered, thought there might be exceptions that require pre-rendering (e.g. a video call of a distant location during combat SQ42 might be better to be streamed from disk for optimization purposes).
Some secondary views are very achievable whereas others would be far too costly/complex. For example, a landing camera on a ship (to ensure you're not squashing someone) could be quite cheap if it was more of a LIDAR/depth-detection camera that was limited to a short range, but if instead it was a full HDR render of the scene including all planets, gas clouds and other ships then you're basically rendering the whole game twice, and reducing resolution doesn't always save you that much (there are many other bottlenecks that are still present at low resolution). A landing camera also has the benefit that the content under your ship is likely already streamed in, whereas a camera on a distant planet would be impossible as you'd need to generate & stream that planet first (at huge cost considering you could be stood on another planet at the time). A related consideration is that the artists need to budget for secondary cameras, for example a security camera that can be access from another point in a ship/building is fine because we can ensure the room with the monitor and/or the camera is cheap enough to afford the extra overhead. But custom player positioned cameras could be much more costly.
Updating at 1Hz doesn't immediately help us that much, as that would just produce a big frame-time spike once per frame. To make this work we'd need to split the cost over many frames, which is possible but would rule out using some post effects which can't easy be amortized (but these shouldn't really be needed for misc secondary cameras).
