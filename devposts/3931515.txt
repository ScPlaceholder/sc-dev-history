I can't speak for anybody else, but I look through Ask the Devs threads at least once a week these days, even though I rarely respond directly to anything.
One contributing factor to this is that writing up a reply takes significantly more time and effort than reading the post in the first place, and rarely is the answer as simple or clear as a yes/no response.
In order for me to feel comfortable answering a question here, I need all of the following things to be true:
- I have the answer to the question.
- I have some actual authority on the subject in question - I don't want to speak for the work of other teams, both because I don't want to seem to take credit for their hard work and because I don't want to inadvertently commit them to some preconception as to what they will be delivering.
- The answer is not a secret - there's a balance to open development that is difficult to articulate, but it 100% does not work to just say everything being considered or developed at all times, and it definitely seems like a sprinkling of surprises both big and small lead to healthier and happier engagement. That may just be how it looks to me, as a person who fills my down-time crawling through spectrum and reddit and youtube and twitch, and again I want to be clear that I am only speaking for myself here.
- All of the above is also true for any critically relevant context required to properly communicate the answer to the question - even in a space game, nothing gets done in a vacuum, and content and features are always linked to each other in some way. Providing only half that information can risk all manner of miscommunication.
- I have the time and energy to answer the question - I don't count the time I spend reading through reddit or spectrum threads as part of my work day, and I have a hard time believing that people would want me to. My job is to work on the game, and so I do that. All of my community interaction that doesn't take place in front of a camera or on a stage is from my own free time.
- I have the confidence and emotional fortitude to commit to answering the question - miscommunication might be better than no communication for most backers, but every time I've woken up to find my name in a the title of a big reddit thread it's had me breaking out in hives. Anxiety hives, like those videos of somebody just carrying a fistful of bees around. Or getting carried by the bees. And some of the bees are spiders. The point is, while I've never been admonished for my interactions with the community, and there are some really positive experiences I have had doing so, those interactions are always bundled up with a measure of risk - it's hard sitting down at the table for conversation when you know that some of these tables have bombs under them, and it's not always easy to know which ones.
Trivial engagement is the easiest and safest way to say, 'hey, we're here, we're listening to what people have to say and watching the cool stuff the community is making and doing, we're human people who are excited about video games and art and we appreciate you being human people who are excited about video games and art.'
I can appreciate how this might not be a satisfying response, particularly since it doesn't include any plans or ideas for how to provide you with more of the kind of engagement you're looking for, but at least I hope it provides some insight to hear about it from my perspective. I love this game and this community, and it can be frustrating to not be able to openly share everything all the time, especially when a discussion involves only some of the parts of that list above - just as I know it must be frustrating to have a good or simple question and seem unable to get a straight answer on it - but ultimately my priority is on making my best contributions to the game and to the rest of the team making it with me.
