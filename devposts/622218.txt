I plan on doing an article specifically on this, but essentially, with names almost anything goes. You can use an epenthetic vowel after an illegal consonant in coda. Saint could produce Sēn’tâ or you could write it with vowel muting to produce S’ēnt, but they'd likely pronounce it as Sēn’tâ anyway. My name is spelled with a muted vowel on the initial P (Pri’tân or Pri’tan), but they'd likely pronounce that as P.âr’i’tan. The Xi’an use nicknames, so they could give you a Xi’an name, but it might be a bit strange to give YOURSELF a Xi’an name. The other thing you can do is take your name and put it in the phonology of another human language that goes better into Xi’an as a transitional step: Saint >> Santo (es/it) >> San’to. Richard >> Richard (fr) >> R.ix’ār. You can also chop off sounds and keep it "close but not perfect." England >> IIng’len, IIng’lan.
