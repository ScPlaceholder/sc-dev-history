Most often we use the term "diegetic" to describe a game element that exists within the fiction rather than outside of it.
The term was borrowed from film making where diegetic and non-diegetic sound are common terms.  In a movie, an example of non-diegetic sound would be the film score. This is music that the viewer can hear, but the characters in the story do not. A diegetic sound example would be music coming from a radio in a scene where both the people watching and the characters in the film hear the music. For a slightly longer definition you can visit here: http://filmsound.org/terminology/diegetic.htm
This term translates well to video games to help clarify between game elements that the player can see and hear, verses elements that their avatar can see and hear in the game.
A common usage example in games is with diegetic vs non-diegetic UI. Often in games, when you bring up an inventory menu screen, your character in game doesn't see it. Likewise, the health bar on your screen is for the player only and not something the character is aware of. These would be non-diegetic UI. However, in Star Citizen, by placing these elements into your helmet visor and onto your character's mobiGlas they become part of the fictional world, or in other words, diegetic.
