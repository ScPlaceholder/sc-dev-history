Well, the truth is, it doesn't make good physical sense.  If you have one action that stops a ship after a 90 degree yaw leaves it traveling at max velocity on the x axis, and you have another action where a ship can accelerate to maximum velocity on the x axis, there is nothing to differentiate between these two actions.  They are both a delta v change of SCM speed generated by x axis thrusters.  But one is a "manuevering action" and the other is a "strafing action".  If you can use thrust to achieve one action, you can use it to achieve the other.  This is why it's very difficult to isolate maneuvering thrust from strafe thrust in coupled mode.  I really haven't come up with a good way to justify it.  It's just that with the current system, IFCS has thrust allocated for individual kinds of control actions, and it can treat a maneuvering action as different from a strafe action, setting aside different amounts of thrust for each, and thus supporting different levels of acceleration for each.  So if we do it, it'll basically be a function of the control system rather than a physical limitation.
