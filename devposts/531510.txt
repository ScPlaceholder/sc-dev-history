I understand that, but consider that if you split the work for one frame onto, say, five GPUs, when they finish that work they need to deliver their results. So if you stuck all your shadow rendering on one of them, for instance, you've saved loads of time on the main GPU but you might find that GPU sitting idle while it waits for the shadow data to trickle back over. Discrete GPUs could be good for some types of work, eg runtime-generated static resources, you could spend several frames making them really good and not worry about finishing and delivering them in less than 1/60 of a second... but such a feature would have to be designed in a way that somehow gets worthwhile benefits on high-quality mode, yet runs fast and looks good enough on a single GPU. We have some work that could theoretically be offloaded already, like generating environment probes, but the overall speedup of offloading them like that is probably less than the speedup of traditional alternate-frame rendering.
