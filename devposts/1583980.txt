it really is just a question of quality. it's incredibly difficult to make a dry weapon shot sound like it's been fired in a specific space, just by processing it. like taking a dry weapon sound and trying to make it sound like it has been fired in a canyon. it just doesn't work!
we do use the systemic reverb system supportively, but everything in the game is sent to the game-defined reverbs in some way or another.
film is film, and comparing the sound approach on a game, to the sound approach on a film, unfortunately is quite impossible in my opinion. in a movie, you might hear a gun being fired once, maybe a few times, but it is absolutely never as constant as hearing a weapon being fired in FPS gameplay. that said, I 100% guarantee most large scale Hollywood movies strive to get original recordings of weapons to suit the spaces in the film - and even if they just have to cut from libraries, they'd much preference cutting from source material that suits the acoustic location, rather than trying to use algo or convol verb in the daw.
there's just something significantly more convincing about using real material vs trying to fake it with plugins!
