This is a really hard thing to answer. There are a multitude of ways that a bug might arise and reasons behind them. Very often though with a game like this, it is simply related to scale. Scale of the player base. The scale of certain features. The scale of the codebase and more.
When you consider how many programmers are all working on the same project, at the same time, it's almost inevitable that one change causes an issue in another feature. We have a good number of QAs, and many automated tests, but often the reproduction steps for a bug might involve having to play the game for an hour before it rears its ugly head. It's more likely to show up when we make the build public, because despite how many QA we have, you guys have got us vastly outnumbered.
Regarding auto tests  simulating thousands of people using the freight elevator: as RobotSpartan mentioned, automated tests can be valuable, but are usually quite limited. They generally test code or features in isolation. That helps us catch a lot of issues, but if you want to properly simulate the impact of 1000 people playing the game, you kinda need to have 1000 real instances of Star Citizen up and running. Anything less means it's not a realistic simulation.  The computing power alone is significant. For example, I'm currently writing this while fixing a bug that requires me to have a mini version of the PU server running, along with two debug builds of the client. On my beast of a machine, I'm getting 10fps, and joining on the 2nd client took a good few minutes.
