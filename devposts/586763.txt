I'm going to comment in general on the most important topics here just clumped together. 1. Metaphor, YES. The definitely have it, but they also mark it explicitly with ki’a when it is not slang or not already built into a compound. I didn't realize that this was supposed to be a translation of "hive mind" per se. I'm not sure WHAT I'd do for that. Probably ngiyal e s.o’e ("reciprocal consciousness"). Keep in mind that Xi’an is strictly HEAD INITIAL. All the modifiers come AFTER the thing being modified.
2. The rules about what happen with pitch in compounds are not as strict as they are in Mandarin, for example. There is a general tendency for things in compounds to "pull to the middle." In the word for toilet, for example, the tai « ka” » in its most raw form is true high, but drops to mid-high in ka’uōng. In ha’ha the first ha” drops to mid-high and the 2nd one loses it "emphatic" character altogether. This is not a pure TONE language in the traditional sense.
I have not written up anything highly formal about the interplay of pitch, but would be happy to serve as an informant if any other linguists/conlangers want to volunteer to take that project on and share it with everyone. :slightly_smiling_face:
