- ædzu (utu, kto)		-	n.	-	a type of root that can be powdered and used to thicken food.
- angi (kto, go)		-	n.	-	oil (when edible be careful to classify it with kto).
- aofwingi (kto)		-	n.	-	a general term for “cooking wine” in Banu cuisine. From ao fo wingi.
- aondroko (kto)		-	n.	-	a type of grain-based cooking wine used in Banu cuisine not unlike a rice wine or sakè.
- endanga (utu, kto)		-	n.	-	a fatty fruit with a sweet flavor (like banana mixed with animal fat).
- fuki (fukiyæ)		-	v.T3	-	be spicily aromatic; be peppery .owingi fuFea fuki zó zitatæ ke eto ye ssunji lo. “Fea’s cooking is always utterly bland and not to my taste.”
- ibigundo (utu, kto)		-	n.	-	a bug with a savory and aromatic flavor similar to garlic.
- ndroko (ndroko)		-	v.T7	-	be a grain species of plant comparable to rice. See undroko. See also aondroko.
- ofuki (kto)		-	n.	-	a strong, peppery spice used in Banu cooking. Similar to white pepper.
- oyungo (kto)		-	n.	-	a sweet and pungent vegetable that can be juiced and used in cooking. Similar in character to ginger (but fleshy and not fibrous).
- ozadu (go, ndzo, kto)		-	n.	-	powder; powdery substance. See aksi.
- ubu (utu, kto)		-	n.	-	a type of nut about the size of a walnut that is used to make angi fubu “ubu oil.” See angi.
- ufuki (utu)		-	n.	-	The plant from which ofuki is made. See ofuki. See also fuki.
- undroko (utu)		-	n.	-	the plant (grain) used to make aondoroko. See aondroko.
- upi (kto)		-	n.	-	meat; flesh.
- uyungo (utu)		-	n.	-	the plant that oyungo comes from. See oyungo. See also yungo.
- yungo (yungo)		-	v.T7	-	be of a species of Banu “ginger”.
- zadu (zaduwæ)		-	v.T3	-	be powder; be powdery. .cho la wolana zadu su nja kte lo nyo wingi. “That has to be crushed into powder before it can be used for cooking.”
