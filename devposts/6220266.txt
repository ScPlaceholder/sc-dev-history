I’m not sure I follow your question, so I’ll explain how this works at a developer level in the hope this clarifies things:
In Vulkan there are many “extensions” which allow additional functionality, and several of these relate to ray tracing. Using these extensions allows you a couple of approaches to tracing rays, either “dispatching“ a group of rays, or tracing rays “inline” in traditional shaders. The implementation behind both of those is fully hardware specific, as is the driver side management. You don’t get to see or configure what hardware is used. On newer NVidia cards these features map to the tensor cores, but there’s no opt in/out at the developer level, the only decision is whether we use the ray tracing extensions or not, assuming they are flagged as supported on your hardware.
Using the ray tracing extensions is obviously not the only way to calculate geometric intersection of rays; and we fire thousands of rays on CPU and GPU every frame for a variety of purposes.
Our GI implemention will be described in detail in due course, but I can say that we’re currently tracing rays using both the Vulkan hardware extensions and using our own software implementation.
