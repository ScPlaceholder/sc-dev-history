Depends on the type of QA. An automation engineer is going to need to know how to program, but an entry level QA tester working for a temp agency ( this is how I started my career ) mainly just needs to be good at breaking things.
I started working at Sony and what they did was put 20 of us through a training class for how to QA test games and assigned us to different projects based on what they saw from us during the class. They evaluated us based on our ability to work together, communicate, and find interesting bugs. I managed to break some of their games in ways they hadn't seen before by wiggling my way into a hay bale and using the collision to shoot myself up into the sky. This got me put on the PS3 testing team, testing release titles for the console before it was publicly available.
Frankly, being a temp at a big company sucks because work is inconsistent but it's one of the ways to get a foot in the door into the industry without having prior experience or expertise. I also highly value the learning experiences that I had while being a temp.
Funny thing about that, I tested a title named Warhawk while I was at Sony. One of the programmers on that title is on the same team as me at CIG.
