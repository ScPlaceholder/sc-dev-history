To give some deeper insight into the challenges/work required: Almost every texture in every game you play uses BC texture compression, which doesn’t have a precise bit depth but in practice it’s around 7-8bit. Moving away from this would incur a HUGE memory and performamce hit, and would largely be pointless as the vast majority of surfaces don’t exceed the Rec709 colour space. There are obviously plenty of examples that do exceed the rec709 colour space, many paints and flowers for example, but in these cases they’d still likely be able to use the same ~8 bit texture formats, because in most cases they won’t use all the extremes of the colour space in a single texture, so the available range can be shifted to precisely where it’s needed on a per asset basis.
Even with the best will to support the wide gamut, you first need every art package and art workflow to support it, train the artists, and purchase high quality monitors. You also need to figure out what you’ll do on the far more common Rec709 displays, as we don’t want colours to clip, so they need tone mapping somehow.
Lighting is easier to support wide gamut as it doesn’t usually involve textures, and would be great for things like red emergency lighting. But the vast majority of lights would not require wide gamut.
None of this is an argument for not supporting wide gamut, the sample videos I’ve played look truly awesome! But I would be very keen to hear what surfaces/areas you hope could use the wide gamut in Star Citizen, and also any good examples you’ve seen in other games… because nothing comes for free, and every dev hour spent on this would be time not spent on global illumination, optimisation, and other tech we all want to see.
