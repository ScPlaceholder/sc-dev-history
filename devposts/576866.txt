I didn't do a whole lot of this, but there are hints of the evolution in the special relative marker se being used for groups and showing up in the pronouns that skew plural. The Service dialect pronouns also show pluralization in their .M (in coda) coming from m.oa being used as a generalization marker. There are also changes CURRENTLY going on in the language. There is a blurring of L and R in speech and somewhat in the orthography and also in the writing system they have formalized XY into a single letter. That has not happened yet for CHY (even though both sounds are subject to the same phonological rules when they bump into I and II and palatalized vowels and diphthongs). The "beast" slang phrase is pronounced e ji gao” jho ma’ma in the Service dialect and all of the pronouns are distinct in that dialect. It is a diachronically "light" approach vis-à-vis evolution. I imagine that there are other languages and dialects of Proper Xi’an across the empire. For example, the UEE likely picked up the name Kayfa from a blurred K/G pronunciation at the beginning plus the cluster BVA (P voiced to B, U ››› V) in the second syllable as the Service dialect has it. Humans heard this as FA. In the Proper language they'd pronounce it Kai’pua, but they actually officially call it by a different name. I'm not sure I can share that yet. :wink: There are other planet names (as known to the UEE) that are based on early contact with speakers of other dialects. I don't have a full list of all that yet. There are also animal names in the Proper language that reflect regional origins. kar’tu is a wasp-like insect (a dire fly). The Proper language would have it as kal’tu. When marketing to the Humans they went with (what seemed to Aopoa) the “more robust” or “no-nonsense”-sounding branding. The Service dialect tends to voice everything and has DR where the Proper language has THL. That's about it (so far).
