1) The initial projectile that contains the noise charge will inherit the ships velocity. Though after it exploded the field will stay in place.
2) Decoys are strong, omni-directional signature sources. The spoofing is based on angular offsets to the target and the relative signal strengths.
3) If you come from a sim background, the word actively sounds stronger than it is. Spoken in code terms a noise field is effectively a limited space in which contained signatures are dampened down by a certain degree. So that process can be described as active. However it does not mean the same as active jamming in the real world. While we might have the equivalent of e-warfare in the future we are not trying to directly emulate modern day ECM weaponry (or sensors) that air forces have in their inventory.
4) I assume you are asking for the logic in the lore: Missiles give off quite a big signature which the ship radar (which is strictly speaking not a radar but a sensor fusion display showing detected EM, CS and IR signatures) can easily pick up ... the missile warning system can then figure out if the missile is intended for your or not. Based on the detected missile signature itself it is able to match it against the catalogue of known missiles and figure out the type. The ship shouldn't actually tell you about these things before the incoming missile is ignited.
