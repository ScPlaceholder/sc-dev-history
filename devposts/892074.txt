From a tech perspective, it's kind of a funny thing - I'm told it's a pretty new thing to be doing, but mechanically it's been possible since before normal maps were even used, even before texturing actually.
So, the standard authoring process (in MAX at least) is that you set up a bunch of smoothing groups on the model, then a normal is generated on each vertex as a weighted average of the normals of the polygons around it. Along the edges of smoothing groups, vertices get duplicated for each smoothing group and get different normals, giving you a hard crease.
Custom normals just gives the artist a lot more control over the normal generation process - a common usage, for example, would be if you have a sharp 90-degree edge, you can bevel it by moving them apart slightly and adding a thin strip of polygons in between. Using custom normals for that means that the vertices at the edge of the bevel can stay perfectly aligned to the original face, preventing it from looking curved, but the interpolated normals on the bevel will look like a very clean curve from one to the other.
