Yeah, that makes sense... you can easily describe every position from a central star by knowing your own pitch and yaw offset based on a galactic direction frame (so the anchor point is the galactic core). Similar how you can specify your position on a map when you found a POI and have a compass. And that is also the problem with using the Sun as a north pole on your direction frame ... because if ships are spread out around the star at an equal distance, all compasses would all point in the same direction (towards the star) so they could never find each other. It is different to using a flat map on a planet where the North pole is technically a POI as well but in practise it really is a directional anchor point.
So the question really is what the usage of our space compasses is. Finding somebody in a star systems without a marker pointing there is a different requirement than just letting somebody know that there is an incoming target from a specific direction. So feel free to expand here what kind of use cases for a space compass you'd like to see.
