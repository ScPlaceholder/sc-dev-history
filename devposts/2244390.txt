Sure ... so essentially whenever you turn on head tracking in SC or press the recalibrate button, SC will use the current raw data of TrackIR and treat it as the new center. So any head movement you do after that is moved relative to that new center point. Check my awesome coder-art™ picture for details. The green circle on the left represents the current TrackIR output on e.g. the yaw axis. Using the default SC head tracking setup that green circle will now be treated as the new center point. That means effectively in SC the TrackIR curve will have a slightly shift.
So while that introduces an offset, it is not really noticable during gameplay (if its not too big). Especially when you switch head tracking on / off regularly or primarily use the hold function it is not obvious. And it has the benefit that the user does not need to constantly take care to keep her / his own head straight and in place before the recalibration.
However that may fall apart when your TrackIR profile has a big dead zone ... because the offset is shifting that dead zone and this may create an imbalance of your axes. So that is why the "raw options" have been added. You do not get the benefits of the automated head calibration but it just uses the pure raw data from TrackIR ... and requires you use the TrackIR recalibrate button.
