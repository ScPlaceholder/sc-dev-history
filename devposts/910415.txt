You're spot on. The more features we add to the loading process, the more we have to consider the landing pad itself. If we require a cargo deliver/collection point the player needs to physically take cargo to/from then that needs to be present on every cargo-capable landing pad. From Port Olisar to a tiny science outpost. If we just spawn the cargo next to the ship and say "there you go, now load that" we may as well just auto load the ship outright. If we have the cargo spawn some distance from the ship, so it feels like we're taking it from the supplier and delivering it to the cargo hold, it feels more like you're loading up a ship but the travel distance is greatly increased, and so is the load time, making a fun little task into a huge chore with little to no gameplay appeal. Casting the right tone of "is this still fun?" against "does this compliment the feature?" will be a constant balancing act.
People in this thread have said they literally don't have the time to dedicate to loading their ship themselves and would pay in-game money to let someone/something else load their ship for them. This leads to another, less exciting issue of production: What is the real demand for this feature? What happens if only a small group of hardcore players are the only ones who actually want to load cargo manually more than once or twice and the rest want to unload quickly, make their money and get back onto the road where the adventure is? The art, code and design work that goes into a feature like this will be many man-months of work, regardless of the final direction we go in. Landing Zones and Space Stations may need to be reworked to accommodate delivery spots or extra space carved out of landing platforms to make space for cargo. Can we justify that work if almost no-one will use it. Is that development time going to be at the expense of something else?
