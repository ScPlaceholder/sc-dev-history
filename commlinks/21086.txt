DefenseCon New Ships
There's a full payload of new ships dropping this DefenseCon, and we know you want that sweet, sweet intel.
We hit up the Vehicle and Gameplay teams to fire off the hard-hitting questions about every ship blasting onto the scene at DefenseCon 2956. Here are the answers, straight from the devs themselves.
DRAKE IRONCLAD & IRONCLAD ASSAULT
How have the Ironclad and Ironclad Assault evolved since they were first concepted, and how have other recent flyable ships informed their development?
Since their concept, the primary change to both Ironclads has been the internal layout in the rear half of the ship, prioritizing ease of traversal throughout what is a complex environment. The original concept interior had a lot of back-and-forth traversal across the width of the ship. Now, there are additional stairwells and ladders to help cut down movement time.
Other changes occurred in the hold, which resulted in increased cargo capacity by adjusting the cargo grid size to allow for more space utilization while maintaining a clear path through the center.
Both the Ironclad and Ironclad Assault appear to be well equipped for multiple roles beyond their intended use as armored freight and combined-arms platform respectively. What types of multi-crew gameplay do you see them excelling at?
Both platforms have the capacity to go well beyond their intended roles simply due to their cargo areas’ sheer size and volume. For example, other vehicles can be stowed within the cargo hold, allowing the Ironclads to serve as motherships or hubs for other gameplay loops.
With the release of the Ironclad, the Caterpillar will also receive its Command Module. While the Ironclad’s module can be used with the Caterpillar, is the reverse also true, and how does the module differ from the original Caterpillar concept?
Both ships now share an identical Command Module. When making the current Command Module, we wanted to ensure the role of the previous one was maintained for Caterpillar owners, hence why it still has the beds and drop seats. So, regardless of which ship it’s attached to, it can function as the “life raft” as originally intended.
Can you tell us more about the Ironclad Assault’s fabricator? What can it craft, and how does it fit into the ship's broader utility role?
Like the Clipper, the Ironclad Assault features a fabricator, and when available in a future patch, it will be able to craft any available blueprint directly to and from the cargo grid. However, it is not enabled at launch due to the functionality of fabricating to and from vehicle grids not currently being available.
The Ironclad includes multiple tractor beams. Are they all the same, or do they differ in functionality?
They are all the same size but, due to their locations and ability to be moved (much like the RSI Hermes’ tractor rail), they provide multiple angles of coverage for loading and unloading.
How does the Ironclad Assault’s repair and refuel functionality work, and can it be used on small ships as well as ground vehicles?
Like other select ships in Alpha 4.8, the Ironclad Assault features the initial implementation of Ship Hangar Services. This is currently ship-wide, so any ground vehicle or ship within the Ironclad Assault can utilize it. However, in a future patch, this will be limited to the rear third of the cargo bay, which is the designated repair area.
It was previously mentioned that the Ironclad could not be flown independently with the Command Module detached, although the main hull would still retain some core functions such as power, cooling, tractor-beam control, and self-defense. How do you envision pilots making use of these capabilities in-game when the command module is detached?
The ability to leave behind the main hull allows for easier atmospheric entry and exit while leaving the parent ship above the planet to carry on doing whatever activities are required.
How will insurance claims work when the command module is detached and the main ship is lost or destroyed?
If the main ship is lost or destroyed, the claim will return the host vehicle with a new Command Module attached, effectively claiming both at once. Any previously stored Command Module from that ship will be bricked, whether it was stored at the time of the claim or not. Bricked modules remain visible in the Fleet Manager and can be removed from the ASOP through the new scrap option.
If the loadout is insured, players can select the insured loadout option when filing the claim. This returns both the Command Module and the host vehicle with a snapshot of the prior loadout changes applied.
The Command Module can also be claimed on its own, without triggering a claim on the host vehicle.
With ship armor now implemented, how does this system affect the Ironclad and Ironclad Assault's performance and role in the broader fleet, and how do they sit within the landscape of ships like the M2, C2, and Starlancer series?
With armor now active, the Ironclad takes its place as a durable industrial hauler, plated for sustained industrial operations. The Ironclad Assault carries improved armor on top of that, letting it endure stressful situations better than the civilian variant.
Looking at armor capabilities, the Ironclads sit in the middle of the pack, better protected than lighter civilian haulers, like the C2 Hercules and Starlancer, but below more refined military platforms, like the M2 Hercules. Even so, both Ironclad variants carry more base hull HP, letting them keep soaking up damage long after lighter rivals would be disabled, extending their operative time in hostile space.
DRAKE PITBULL
Can you give us an overview of the Pitbull’s armaments and what kind of targets it was built to take on?
In classic Drake form, the Pitbull has more guns than comparable craft, providing plenty of firepower at the expense of durability.
The Pitbull joins a competitive snub fighter category alongside ships like the Mirai Fury. What sets it apart?
The Pitbull trades resilience for aggression. Where the Fury concentrates its fire through 4× S2 guns, the Pitbull spreads it across 6 barrels (4× S1 plus 2× S2), putting more rounds on target at the cost of per-shot punch. Built on a lighter frame, it pitches harder, accelerates faster, and turns tighter in the close fight but pays for that edge with a more fragile hull that cannot trade hits.
How many Pitbulls can the Ironclad Assault carry, and what does that open up for groups running them together?
Simply put, lots. With some careful arrangement, it can easily be into double figures, allowing for the Ironclad to become a snub carrier.
With Tactical Strike Groups releasing alongside the Pitbull, how does the ship's hit-and-run design philosophy translate into that gameplay?
While not packing heavy firepower to damage larger ships, a swarm of Pitbulls is a great hit-and-run deterrent to help screen larger ships and provide complementary fire support.
In addition to dogfighting and swarm tactics, the Pitbull’s minimalist approach and high speed seem like they’d make for a competent racer. Is this something the team considered during production?
Naturally, given their size and relative agility, most snubs suit racing, but the Pitbull is not specifically tailored to it.
As a snub fighter lacking a quantum drive, the Pitbull was clearly designed to be carried within a larger vessel. Which ships was the Pitbull designed to fit within?
Part of the initial brief criteria for the Pitbull was to not exceed the volume of the Fury to maximize transport suitability, so any ship that can fit a Fury should be capable of fitting a Pitbull. A great thematic example is the Drake Ironclad Assault, as this allows both the storage of multiple Pitbulls as well as Ship Hangar Services, providing fleet support to other operations, such as mining or escort.
ORIGIN M80
Origin has a rich history in racing and luxury. What inspired the team to expand into the Heavy Fighter category with the M80?
While Origin typically evokes thoughts of racing and luxury, it’s not exclusive to those, with ships like the M50 being used for Interceptor roles and the G12a being a combat variant of the base G12. When looking at developing a new combat ship, we wanted to expand the lineup with something unlike any existing Heavy Fighter, so Origin was a natural fit.
What kind of playstyle is the M80 designed for? What role or situations will it excel in?
Although a Heavy Fighter, the M80 features a dedicated 2 SCU cargo grid housed within the body, allowing players to use this ship for more than pure combat missions. The addition of a simple (for Origin standards) interior bed allows players to use it for logging out away from their home locations.
How will the M80 stand up against other Heavy Fighters?
Compared to other Heavy Fighters, the M80 is not lacking in firepower, featuring both S4 and S5 guns. It packs a concentrated punch against heavier targets rather than an overwhelming volume of lighter fire, as per some heavy fighters.
How did the team approach balancing agility for this ship, and how does that translate to what pilots will actually feel in the cockpit compared to other Heavy Fighters?
The M80 carries Origin's racing heritage into the Heavy Fighter category, with top-end speed that outpaces the vast majority of the lineup, sitting just behind the Scorpius series. Pilots will feel this when closing on a target or breaking away, where the M80 is quicker than its peers. To balance that pace and its heavy weapon loadout, the M80 trades pitch and yaw response for snappier roll authority. Most importantly, it runs a noticeably lighter and more fragile hull, the key trade-off that defines its character.
Origin is known for bespoke features. Are there any features about the M80 that stand out?
Relative to other Origin ships, the M80 is modest in feature set, pairing heavy firepower in a single seater with a small cargo capacity to provide flexibility.
Origin ships are precision engineered down to the last detail. How does that philosophy show up in the M80's weapons and defense?
One of the signature elements of the M80 is its part-hidden weapon loadout, with the main S5 guns deploying from the body when powered on, changing both the silhouette and combat profile of the ship.
MISC STARLITE
Can you walk us through the process of refueling?
Refueling recently received an update that will slightly change the way it works. Overall, it will require less input, though it still has some important management in the background that players can optimize.
The process is that the refueling ship owner first enters Refueling mode, which will automatically extend the fuel nozzle. While the fuel nozzle is extended, any incoming or outgoing docking request is interpreted as a refueling offer or request. Accepting this will trigger the docking process. Once docked, the player asking for fuel can open their mobiGlas to order. The refueling ship owner then has to make sure that the relevant fuel pods are open. If they are already open, refueling will start and complete automatically. The player can influence refueling speed by having the correct pods open that benefit fuel flow. Refueling players should pay attention to the new pods being introduced to the in-game shops!
What makes the Starlite different to the Starfarer? What makes them similar?
Compared to the Starfarer, the biggest separator is fuel capacity. While they share the same pods (allowing interoperability between the two), the Starfarer carries twice the capacity of the Starlite.
Is the Starlite best suited to small-to-medium ships, or can it service larger vessels?
It’s better suited to smaller ships. Larger ones can be serviced, albeit with a lower fuel capacity, so it’s not as effective as the Starfarer at larger jobs.
Can the MISC Starlite refuel itself?
Not from the fuel pods themselves, but another Starlite or Starfarer can refuel it.
With this being the first entry-level refueler, how will players grow with this ship?
Though entry-level, the only limitation with the refueling loop is the total capacity of fuel aboard. The Starlite is fully compatible with the same alternative nozzle upgrades as the Starfarer.
How profitable will refueling be compared to salvaging, hauling, and mining?
We treat refueling as a full, viable profession. While players should be able to earn a living in the 'verse with every profession we introduce, we acknowledge that this sort of balancing is a complex and ongoing process that will evolve with the game.
With the introduction of the Starlite, will we see more refueling missions?
Yes. With Alpha 4.8, we’re introducing specific missions to enhance the Refueling career.
What can pilots expect when it comes to handling and steering?
The Starlite is positioned as an introduction to refueling, with handling tuned for accessibility over raw performance. Forward thrust is responsive and lateral and vertical movement are tweaked down to allow more forgiving adjustment maneuvers. Pilots can expect a stable, predictable ride that suits the measured pace of refueling work.
With improved docking coming to the 'verse, how will this impact the Starlite?
The Starlite will benefit from the improved docking functionality, just like other ships that refuel or use docking, be it host-to-parasite or ship-to-station docking.
AEGIS TIBURON
With the Perseus, Hammerhead, and Tiburon forming a trio of heavy gunships, how does each fill a distinct role, and where does the Tiburon carve out its own identity?
Each of the Heavy Gunships occupies a different profile: the Perseus is designed for engaging large sub-capital threats, the Hammerhead is an anti-fighter, and the Tiburon takes on capital ships with its combination of S10 beam weapon and primarily forward-facing S7 ballistic turrets.
The Tiburon's Supremacy-10T Laser Beam is hard to miss. Can you tell us more about this signature weapon?
The weapon is made by Hurston Dynamics and, like the Exodus, is a custom integration into the chassis.
The Tiburon is similar in size to the Hammerhead, but with the laser beam weapon filling the center of the hull. How does this additional mass affect its flight performance?
The Tiburon is built around a weapon powerful enough to threaten capital-class ships, and the rest of the hull gives way to that. Flight performance pays the price, sitting below the Hammerhead's, with slower response and a heavier maneuvering feel; this is the cost of carrying so much firepower into a fight.
The Tiburon runs two Size 3 powerplants to power its components, turrets, and laser beam. How does this setup meet the ship's demanding power needs in combat, and inform its fighting style?
Compared to the Hammerhead, which runs an identical power loadout for 24 smaller weapons versus a single S10 weapon, it’s similar. However, the initial design acknowledged a power limitation, hence the decision to use ballistic weapons on the remote turrets for both power and anti-capital-ship role reasons.
With a laser beam built for taking on large ships, how does the Tiburon fare against swarms of smaller ships?
The Tiburon retains the quad manned turret at the rear of the Hammerhead, but it’s limited in its coverage and more of a defensive measure than an offensive one. The remote turrets pack a hefty blow to anything caught in their path, though they’re not as effective at countering smaller ships.
How does the Tiburon hold up defensively?
Like other Heavy Gunships, the Tiburon features extensive and durable armor as its main protection point.
How feasible is it to fly the Tiburon solo, and how does it balance for multi-crew gameplay?
The main beam can be pilot-controlled, so while it is possible to fly solo, the Tiburon will be wide open to attack from every other angle.
The Tiburon's remote turret guns look integrated into the turrets themselves. Can you tell us what weapons they are, and are they bespoke or swappable?
The S7 ballistic cannons featured on the remote turrets are identical to the Behring guns featured on the Idris-T’s main turret kit.
The Tiburon has a pointed bow. Where does the docking airlock go?
The Tiburon does not have a docking port at all because of the structural changes to support the Supremacy-10T weapon.
With a second laser beam weapon now in the game, are there plans to introduce them in other sizes?
Yes, although we currently don’t plan to introduce them in the smaller-size weapon classes, instead retaining them for use on larger ships.
