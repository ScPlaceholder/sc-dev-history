IAE 2951 – Test The Limits
The galaxy's premier annual aerospace event, the Intergalactic Aerospace Expo (IAE), runs from November 19 to December 1.
To commemorate the event, we want to spotlight the milestones of spaceflight. Pilots have always dared to risk the cold, harsh expanse of space, soaring above worlds with no safety net to catch them. Spaceflight is about being bold and doing what needs to be done.
Find out more about the IAE 2951 here!
This week, let’s take a look at another key player in history, Michelle Saleno.
During the mid-2130s, Roberts Space Industries began testing the Zeus prototype, a short-range explorer that would eventually make history as the first commercially available spaceship. In 2136 during a flight that was being broadcast across the world, the hull of an early Zeus ship ripped apart as it left Earth’s atmosphere, destroying the ship.
RSI turned to the navy and renowned test pilot Michelle Saleno for help in keeping the Zeus program alive. Saleno, affectionately called Sal by her friends, had extensive experience with RSI’s Quantum Core engine and held the honor of being the first pilot to quantum past Jupiter.
Saleno and her crew spent the first year demanding changes before she or any of her pilots would take a first test flight. She battled with RSI executives over the ship’s hull and drove RSI to completely overhaul its design. Finally, on 2137-3-19, Saleno climbed inside the Zeus for her first test flight. The new Zeus had been worth the wait. At 15:09 SET, Saleno safely touched down and became a media sensation.
Famously she stated: “A test pilot’s job isn’t about taking risks; it’s about achieving results. It’s about safely landing again just as much as it is about fearlessly flying forward.”
